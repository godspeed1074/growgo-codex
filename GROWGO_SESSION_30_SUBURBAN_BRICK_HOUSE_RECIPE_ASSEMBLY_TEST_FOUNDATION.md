## 6. Assembly Plan

### Footprint size

- width: `10m-13m`
- depth: `12m-15m`
- lot type: standard suburban residential lot

### Road-facing direction

- primary facade must face the street frontage
- the house should present the entry, garage, and front-yard hierarchy toward the road
- the front elevation should preserve GrowGo’s north-facing camera readability

### Garage placement

- preferred placement: garage offset to one side of the front facade
- alternate placement: slightly recessed integrated garage for wider lots
- the garage should never dominate the full facade width unless later recipe variants explicitly call for it

### Driveway placement

- driveway should connect from road frontage directly to the garage socket
- preferred side: same side as the garage
- driveway should remain visually distinct from the entry path

### Front entry

- entry should be clearly visible from the road-facing elevation
- preferred placement: central or opposite the garage side to balance the suburban facade
- entry should connect to a dedicated suburban path, not rely only on driveway access

### Window placement

- large front living-room windows should sit on the most public side of the front elevation
- standard windows should support bedrooms and side elevations
- front-window placement must balance the garage mass and preserve a family-home read

### Fence boundary

- front boundary fencing should remain low, partial, or decorative
- side and rear boundary fencing can be more continuous
- the fence should reinforce lot identity without hiding the house facade

### Garden placement

- front garden should sit between frontage path and lawn edges
- shrub and flowerbed placement should soften the house corners and driveway edge
- garden placement must preserve clear entry readability

### Landscaping

- lawn should be the dominant foreground surface
- one tree may sit in the front yard or verge, with optional second tree in side or rear placement for future variants
- suburban plant placement should feel trimmed and structured rather than coastal-wild

#### 1. Brick wall

- defines the primary suburban facade identity
- highest cross-family reuse across suburban and civic recipes

#### 2. Tiled roof

- creates immediate silhouette separation from coastal gable-family houses
- high reuse across residential and civic families

#### 3. Garage module

- makes the house read unmistakably suburban from the street
- unlocks driveway-facing family-home layouts

#### 4. Standard residential window

- fills the broader suburban facade and bedroom-window need
- useful across future residential recipes

#### 5. Suburban entry path

- improves front-yard organization and lot readability

#### 6. Letterbox

- adds strong suburban character, but it is not a core structural blocker
